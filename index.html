The report

The report is the job. Sayt does it on site.

The walk is the easy part. The cost is the evening afterwards — matching photos to notes, retyping the same findings, chasing the format your client, your head office or the main contractor expects. Sayt removes that evening.

Everything you record on site is already report-shaped, so generating the document is one screen and one tap. Your name, licence number, company and logo are on it. You can send it before you get back to the car.

  • PDF — ready to send, no formatting needed
  • Word (DOCX) — editable in Word or Pages, for teams whose template must be followed
  • Photos (ZIP) — the original images at full quality
  • Photos, annotations, measurements, plan pins, checklists and issue history all pulled in automatically
  • Preview it before you generate it

A week at a time

One report for the whole week, or the whole team

Clients rarely want a document per walk. Pick this week, last week, or any two dates, and Sayt merges every visit in that range into one report — renumbered in order, each item stamped with the day it was recorded so nothing loses its place.

It works across people as well as days. Everyone on the job types the same team code — no accounts, nothing sent anywhere, the code itself is what makes it one job — and their visits come back into it, renumbered on arrival so five people who each recorded “Visit 1” do not collide.

The Team screen shows who has sent what and when they were last on site, takes in a batch of files at once, and turns the lot into one report that says who recorded each finding.

Catching up

The walk you did not write up yet

Nobody documents everything on site. You photograph the job, and the write-up happens that evening, or on Monday, or when the client finally asks. Sayt is built for that instead of pretending otherwise.

Write observations up whenever you get to them. Log a visit that already happened and upload the photos you took on the day — they keep their real capture date, because that date is the evidence, not the moment you typed the note.

  • Write up photos days later without losing when they were taken
  • Log a past visit and upload that day’s photos to it
  • A visit recorded long after the fact says so on the report, plainly
  • Past visits are listed by their date, not squeezed into the numbering

Sayt states what happened and leaves the judgement to you. A write-up done long after the walk is disclosed on the report itself, and that disclosure cannot be switched off. If a photo's capture date does not match the visit you are filing it under, Sayt says so at the time, while you can still pick a different one.

Measure

Dimensions without a tape

Tap Measure on a finding and point the camera at what you need sized. Set the two ends and Sayt reads the distance off the camera — a ceiling height, a crack, the width of an opening — then writes it onto the finding, as shown here.

Chain several lines around an opening, a floor or a patch of damage. Close the shape and Sayt calculates the enclosed area, so "how much render needs replacing?" is answered on site rather than estimated later.

  • Lengths and enclosed areas, both saved to the finding
  • On iPhones with LiDAR, Sayt ranges against real geometry — noticeably more accurate on irregular surfaces. Without it, it measures against detected planes
  • Metric or imperial, switchable at any time
  • Every measurement carries into the report
  • Type one in by hand when you already know the number

The ruler

A tape measure drawn onto the wall

Point the phone and Sayt lays a graduated ruler along whatever you measured, with the graduations numbered like a real tape. Each side of a chained shape keeps its own length, so a window comes back as four figures rather than one.

It tells you what it has locked onto — Vertical surface or Horizontal surface — so you know the reading is coming off the wall you meant, not the ceiling behind it.

  • Graduated ticks and numbers along every line
  • Each side of a shape labelled with its own length
  • Tap the value to flip between metric and imperial
  • Every figure marked ≈ — an estimate, and it says so

Camera measurements are digital estimates and are marked with ≈. They are accurate enough to size an opening, scope a repair or brief a trade — but they are not survey-grade. Check critical dimensions with a tape before ordering material or cutting anything.

Site plans

Pin the finding to the exact spot

Import the floor plan and tap it to drop a pin where the photo was taken. The reader stops guessing which of four identical units, or which end of a corridor, the crack is in.

Pins are repositionable until you save, so getting it right takes a second. Every pinned plan carries through to the report alongside the photograph and its measurements.

  • Use any plan you can get onto the phone — PDF page or photo
  • One pin per finding, placed by tapping the plan
  • Plan and pin appear in the exported report

Calendar

Every visit, and the next one

The home screen opens on a month calendar marking every day you documented something. Tap any day to see exactly what was recorded on it — the photos, the findings, the measurements — so a job reads back as a storyboard rather than a pile of files. Progress stops being a feeling and becomes a record you can show a client: day one against today.

Schedule the next visit while you are still standing on site. One-off, daily, weekly, fortnightly or monthly, with a reminder before you are due.

Who it's for

If your day ends with a report, it was built for you

Sayt is not an architect's app that other trades can borrow. You pick your role the first time you open it, and the categories, the report title and the words in the document all change to match the work you actually do and the people who actually read it.

Site & junior engineers

Often the person who has to write the daily report — and the person nobody showed how. Sayt supplies the structure: log what you saw as you see it, and the document assembles itself in a format your senior will sign without sending it back.

Civil & structural engineers

Categories that match the pour, not a generic list: earthworks, drainage, concrete, masonry — or foundation, rebar, formwork, column, beam and slab. Measure a section with the camera and the dimension lands in the report with the photo.

QA / QC checkers

The Punchlist table template gives you the numbered No. | Name | Description tabulation with photos embedded. Photograph the punchlist head office sent and Sayt reads it in — then work each item through open, in progress and verified.

Foremen, supervisors & construction managers

Cross-trade categories in one profile: civil, structural, architectural, electrical, mechanical, plumbing, safety. The Progress template turns a photo walk into weekly documentation with barely any typing.

Inspectors & regulators

Health, sanitary, food safety and fire profiles write violations, not "observations", under the right document title — the register your office and the establishment expect.

Safety officers (OSH)

Log hazards against PPE, fall protection, machine guarding, electrical safety, hazardous materials, housekeeping, signage and emergency response — with the photo attached and a required action recorded.

Architects

Layout, dimensions, finishes, doors and windows, ceilings, waterproofing and workmanship. Pin each observation to the floor plan so the client can see where it is without reading a paragraph.

Property inspectors & adjusters

Damage assessment, cause of loss, water, fire and storm damage — or roof, plumbing, HVAC, interior and exterior. The Inspection template leads on before/after evidence.

Checklists

Photograph a punchlist your client or head office sent and Sayt reads it into checklist items or issues — no retyping. Numbered, bulleted and tabular lists are all handled, and you confirm each line before it is imported. Runs on-device. Pro.

Handoff

Send a whole visit to a colleague as a single file. It merges into their projects, renumbered on arrival, so several people reporting on one job consolidate instead of colliding. Pro.

Team

Everyone types the same team code and they are on the same job. Take their visits back in, see at a glance who has sent what, and report on all of it. Their photos, findings and issues merge into the project rather than arriving beside it. Read more. Pro.

Timestamps

Each photo can carry the date and time it was taken, under the image in the app and in the report, in your own time zone. Never burnt into the picture.

Sign-off

Your name, licence number and signature on the sheet — with an optional second “reviewed by” column. Both switch on and off as you export, not months earlier in settings.
